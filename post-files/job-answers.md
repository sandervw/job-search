# Walk us through a production issue you caught before it became a customer problem. Tell us which company you were at, what your role was, what you were monitoring, how you caught it, and what you did.

Pharmacists Mutual Insurance Group, as Senior Software Engineer owning the reporting platform. When I rebuilt it, I added reconciliation checks to every nightly load: row counts + financial totals against the source system, and last_load_timestamps, with alerts on any drift. One morning premium totals were off on about 20 personal policies, MTD total of about 5%. Before anyone had logged in or opened a dashboard, I traced it to an early-firing of a mis-scheduled data load. I had built it so that the system automatically held back the final deployment on mismatched validation, so all I had to do was rerun the job (and fix the schedule), all before executives ever saw a wrong number.

# Describe a technical decision you pushed back on or advocated for. Tell us which company and role this was in, what the tradeoff was, how you made your case, and what the outcome was.

Our reporting vendor collapsed, and the default plan was to buy another one. I advocated building in-house. The tradeoff: upfront build time and full ownership, against another era of vendor lock-in and misconfigured logic. I made the case by documenting what the old vendor actually computed, showing where it was wrong, and scoping a phased, continually-running build during the transition. Leadership approved. The rebuilt platform cut storage 50% and processing time 60%, now with documentation which allowed other engineers to contribute meaningful enhancements.

# We use AI coding tools heavily here, Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, and others. Share a workflow, config setup, or file from a personal repo that shows how you've integrated AI tooling into your day-to-day development.

[Dying Skies](https://github.com/sandervw/dying-skies/tree/main/.claude) is a monorepo (React/Canvas frontend, FastAPI/Postgres backend, Dagster/dbt analytics) with a Claude Code setup checked in. The root CLAUDE.md holds the project's standards: file-size caps, naming, docs, and no unrequested features. An `orchestrate` skill splits my build orders into one task per piece, then runs four subagents in stages: a read-only planner, an implementer that self-tests and commits, a validator that checks contracts, and an auditor that checks diffs against the standards. It halts between stages so I can review each one before anything gets pushed to git.
